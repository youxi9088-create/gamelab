    How to play

    Chess made simple

    A quick beginner guide. You move one piece, then your opponent takes a turn.

    A brief history

    Chess is thought to have begun in India around the 6th century as a game called chaturanga, then spread through Persia to Europe. By the late 15th century, European rules had changed dramatically: the queen gained much of its modern power, the bishop acquired its modern long-range diagonal move, and special moves such as castling and en passant took shape. Stalemate also took shape as a recognized outcome during this period, with its status as a draw standardized in the 19th century. Shared rules define legal moves and goals so everyone plays by the same fair standards.

    White always moves first, following the standard 19th-century chess rules.

    1. Your goal

    Trap the enemy king so it cannot escape attack. This is checkmate, and you win.

    2. Take turns

    Tap one of your pieces. Green squares show where it can move. Tap a green square to move. White always moves first, following the standard 19th-century chess rules.

    3. How pieces move

    Pieces cannot jump over other pieces, except the knight.

    PawnMoves forward 1 square (or 2 from its start). Captures diagonally.
    KnightMoves in an L shape and can jump over pieces.
    BishopMoves diagonally any number of clear squares.
    RookMoves straight any number of clear squares.
    QueenMoves straight or diagonally any number of clear squares.
    KingMoves 1 square in any direction. Keep it safe!

    A shared first-pawn choice

    Each side gets one two-square chance: only its first pawn to move may take it. If that pawn moves 1 square, the chance is gone.

    Take the two-square leap

    +2 ↑ +1 ↑ ♙

    Example: your first pawn advances 2 squares. The chance is spent; every later pawn advances 1 square at a time.

    Choose one square

    1♙→+1
    2♙→+1+2

    Example: your first pawn advances 1 square. Your second and every later pawn can then advance only 1 square at a time.

    Castling

    Move the king two squares toward a rook, then move the rook to the square the king crossed.

    You can castle only if the king and rook have not moved, no pieces are between them, and the king is not in check, crosses no attacked square, and ends safe.

    4. Captures and safety

    Capture a piece by moving onto its square. The king is never captured.

    Check

    Your king is under attack. Escape, block the attack, or take the attacking piece.

    How the game ends

    Checkmate means the king cannot escape: you win. Checkmate against you means you lose.

    If a player has no legal move but is not in check, the game is a stalemate, also known as a draw.
